Core formulas

What each engine actually computes — transcribed from the source, with references. Training (analytical) and inference (discrete-event) are modelled separately.

These equations mirror the code (kavier_training/core/engine.py and kavier_inference/stages/), not just the paper. Calibration coefficients (marked calibrated) are fitted on a real fine-tuning trace and stored in kavier_training/data/calibration.json; with calibration off, they default to neutral values.

Training model (analytical)

The training CLI sums four terms into a per-step time, then derives throughput. It powers kavier-train.

1Forward pass time
T_forward = (2 × active_params × batch × seq) / (GPU_FLOP/s × MFU) + overheadcal

Source: Shoeybi et al. (2019) — Megatron-LM; Narayanan et al. (2021)

  • ~2 FLOPs per parameter per token (multiply + add).
  • Uses active_params, so sparse/MoE models scale by per-token cost.
  • overhead is a calibrated per-forward constant.
2Backward pass time
T_backward = 2 × T_forward

Source: Shoeybi et al. (2019); Narayanan et al. (2021) — backward ≈ 2× forward FLOPs

3Optimizer step time (AdamW)
T_optimizer = (20 × trainable_params) / memory_bandwidth

Source: Rajbhandari et al. (2020) — ZeRO; Loshchilov & Hutter (2019) — AdamW

  • Memory-bound: 20 bytes/param moved per step (grad 2 + two moments 4+4, updated moments 4+4, weight 2).
  • trainable_params = all params (full) or 2·r·d·k·L (LoRA, see #14).
4Communication time (ring all-reduce)
T_comm = c_cal × [ latency·log₂N + msg_overhead·(N−1) + grad_bytes·(N−1)/N / bandwidth ]

Source: Thakur et al. (2005); Narayanan et al. (2021)

  • Single GPU → 0. Multi-node adds an inter-node ring over InfiniBand (200 GB/s).
  • grad_bytes = 4 × trainable_params; c_cal is a calibrated scale.
5Step time & throughput
T_step = G_a × (T_forward + T_backward) + T_optimizer + T_comm

Throughput (Ga = gradient-accumulation steps, all scale terms calibrated):

tokens/sec = (G_a × batch × seq × num_GPUs) / (mgc × T_step) × method_scale × model_scale × interaction_scale
  • mgc = multi-GPU correction (per total GPU count); the others adjust per method, per model, and per (model, method, GPU, count).
  • With calibration off, every scale = 1 and mgc = 1 — the pure-physics baseline.

Inference model (discrete-event)

The inference simulator splits each request into prefill and decode and tracks KV-cache memory. It powers kavier-perf. (Compute/memory efficiencies are fixed constants here: COMPUTE_EFFICIENCY = 0.30, MEMORY_EFFICIENCY = 0.60.)

6Prefill time (whole prompt)
T_prefill = overhead + (input_tokens × 2 × params) / (GPU_FLOP/s × COMPUTE_EFFICIENCY)

Source: compute-bound single pass (Vaswani et al., 2017); fixed overhead = 0.025 s.

7Decode time (per token, roofline)
t_token = max( 2·params / (FLOP/s·C_eff) ,  bytes·params / (bandwidth·M_eff) ) T_decode = t_token × n_out  (KV cache on)  |  t_token × n_out·(n_out+1)/2  (off)

Source: memory-bound autoregressive decode (Pope et al., 2022; Kwon et al., 2023 — PagedAttention)

  • Per token is the slower of the compute-bound and memory-bound limits.
  • KV cache changes the token-count: linear with cache, quadratic without (re-reading the whole context each step).
8KV-cache memory (per token)
KV_bytes/token = layers × hidden_dim × 2 × precision_bytes

Source: transformer KV state (Vaswani et al., 2017)

  • 2× for Key and Value; hidden_dim = heads × head_dim.
  • Utilisation = cached tokens × this, over GPU memory.

GPU utilization & MFU

9Roofline (compute vs. memory bound)
throughput = min(peak_FLOP/s, bandwidth × arithmetic_intensity)

Source: Williams et al. (2009) — Roofline

  • Arithmetic intensity = FLOPs / bytes moved; small batches sit in the memory-bound regime.
10MFU (training), batch-scaled
MFU = base_MFU × per_GPU_cal × min(1,  0.0341·log₂(batch) + 0.8147)

Source: Chowdhery et al. (2022) — PaLM; roofline (Williams et al., 2009)

  • Log batch-size scaling captures small-batch underutilisation; capped at the GPU's nominal MFU.
  • per_GPU_cal is fitted per GPU; typical realised MFU is ~15–40% of peak.

Power & sustainability

Energy and CO2 reuse OpenDC's MSE power model — Kavier's implementation matches OpenDC's to within floating-point error, at far higher speed. This drives kavier-energy and kavier-co2 (and the training engine's gpu_power_watts).

11Power draw (OpenDC MSE model)
P(u) = P_idle + (P_max − P_idle) × (2u − u^r)

Source: Fan et al. (2007); Mastenbroek et al. (2021) — OpenDC

  • u = utilisation (max of compute and memory); r a per-GPU calibration exponent.
12Energy & emissions
energy_kWh = P(u) × duration / 3.6×10⁶   CO₂_g = energy_kWh × intensity(t)

Source: Niewenhuis et al. (2024) — FootPrinter

  • Emissions integrate power over time against a time-varying carbon intensity (gCO2/kWh).
  • Efficiency metrics divide by tokens and scale to per-million (see kavier-energy).

Key parameters

Model parameters

  • params: Total trainable parameters
  • layers: Number of transformer layers
  • hidden_dim: Hidden dimension size
  • heads: Number of attention heads

Hardware parameters

  • GPU_FLOPS: Peak TFLOPS (fp16/bf16)
  • memory_bandwidth: GB/s
  • network_bandwidth: GB/s (NVLink, InfiniBand)

Training parameters

  • batch_size: Samples per GPU per step
  • seq_length: Tokens per sample
  • num_GPUs: Total GPUs (data parallel)
  • gradient_accumulation: Steps before optimizer update

Sparse / active-parameter models

Kavier does not track expert structure (no num_experts, no routing overhead term). Sparse architectures are encoded by setting the LLMSpec's active_params to the per-token active subset (e.g. Mixtral-8x7B: m_params=47B, active_params=13B). The forward-FLOP formula (#1) then uses active_params, so compute scales with the effective per-token cost rather than the total weight count. Any residual error is absorbed by the empirical model_scale calibration.

Fine-tuning optimizations

13LoRA / GPTQ-LoRA trainable params
trainable_params = 2 × rank × hidden_dim × target_modules × num_layers

Source: Hu et al. (2021) — LoRA; Dettmers et al. (2023) — QLoRA

  • Defaults in code: rank = 8, target_modules = 4 (Q, K, V, O).
  • Only this count feeds the optimizer (#3) and all-reduce (#4); the forward/backward FLOPs still use the full model — so LoRA mainly cuts optimizer and communication time.

Modelling notes & limits

  1. Two distinct models. Training is analytical (closed-form step); inference is discrete-event (per-request prefill/decode). They share only the GPU/LLM library.
  2. Calibration matters. On the held-out test split, calibration cut the training model's error from ~15% to ~6.2% MdAPE. Coefficients are fitted per GPU/method/model and do not extrapolate to unseen hardware.
  3. Memory vs. compute. The optimizer step and small-batch decode are memory-bandwidth bound; large-batch forward/backward are compute bound (roofline, #9).
  4. Sparse / MoE models are encoded via active_params only — Kavier does not model expert routing or load balancing.
  5. Homogeneous GPUs. The models assume one GPU type per run.
